## Material baseline correction — industrial refrigeration and cold-chain systems

- **What happened:** On 5 November 2026, CISA, FBI, HHS, USDA, and the U.S. Department of Transportation published a joint advisory stating that they were aware of malicious cyber activity targeting electronic temperature controllers and commercial refrigeration controllers in retail grocery and cold-storage facilities worldwide. The advisory says actors may exploit unpatched or internet-accessible controllers or weak configurations to alter temperatures or remove visibility into current temperatures. Potential consequences include equipment hazards, fire, inventory loss, and unsafe products.
- **Who confirmed it:** CISA, FBI, HHS, USDA, and U.S. DOT authored the public advisory. In a separate FBI video published with the alert, FBI Cyber Division Assistant Director Brett Leatherman stated that attackers had targeted refrigeration at three French national blood-service sites earlier in November; alarms reached staff, staff responded, and no blood was lost.
- **Event date vs publication date:** The advisory is marked 'As of November 5, 2026.' It does not give dates for the wider malicious activity. The FBI describes the French attempt only as occurring 'earlier this month'; an exact date was not disclosed. Publication date: 5 November 2026.
- **Affected geography/sectors:** Activity is described as global. The advisory identifies healthcare and public health, chemical, food and agriculture, and transportation systems as sectors that widely rely on these systems. U.S. exposure is relevant because these systems protect food and medicine domestically, but the advisory does not identify a U.S. victim or U.S. service disruption.
- **Classification:** Officially reported malicious cyber activity targeting industrial control equipment. The FBI separately describes an attempted disruption in France. The advisory describes potential attack effects; it does not claim that all listed effects occurred.
- **Likely civilian implications:** If controllers are compromised, temperature excursions or loss of monitoring could damage food and medical inventories, create equipment hazards, or affect product safety. The French blood-service attempt was reportedly detected and handled without blood loss, which is important mitigating evidence.
- **Confidence and unknowns:** High confidence that the five-agency advisory and FBI statement were published. Moderate confidence in the public detail of the French incident: the FBI statement is direct, but the specific event date, facilities, actor attribution, technical method, and scope are not disclosed. The advisory does not quantify affected organizations or report a confirmed U.S. cold-chain outage.
- **Contradictory / mitigating evidence:** In the French case described by the FBI, alarms functioned and personnel responded; no blood was lost. The advisory is preventive guidance and does not establish that all operators or sectors are compromised.
- **Primary sources:** [Joint government advisory (FBI-hosted PDF)](https://www.fbi.gov/investigate/cyber/alerts/2026/us-government-partners-urge-hardening-industrial-refrigeration-and-cold-chain-monitoring-systems); [FBI video and transcript](https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/industrial-refrigeration-and-cold-chain-monitoring-systems.mp4/view).

## New item screened — UK Cyber Adversary Simulation scheme

On 11 November 2026, the UK National Cyber Security Centre announced a Cyber Adversary Simulation (CyAS) scheme to assure providers conducting realistic, customer-specific simulated attacks for critical organisations. It is a cyber-resilience and assurance initiative, initially operating as a minimum viable product. It is not an incident, threat warning, or evidence of an imminent attack, and therefore does not change the threat assessment.

Source: [UK NCSC announcement, 11 November 2026](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/news/ncsc-scheme-help-critical-organisations-test-their-cyber-defences).

## Calibrated assessment

**Localized disruption risk remains the supported category.** Puerto Rico's continuing grid emergency is a documented localized civilian-service risk. The refrigeration advisory documents a separate global cyber threat to systems used in essential supply chains, with a publicly described attempted disruption in France, but no U.S. cold-chain service disruption identified in the advisory. Existing U.S. cyber advisories describe hostile activity and capability, but this pass found no evidence sufficient to establish a broader coordinated destructive campaign or imminent nationwide danger.

Ordinary, proportionate readiness for routine power and supply interruptions remains appropriate. No panic buying or speculative stockpiling is supported.
