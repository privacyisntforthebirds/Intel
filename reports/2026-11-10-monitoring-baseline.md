# Public-source monitoring report — 2026-11-10

**Assessment cut-off:** 10 November 2026 (America/New_York).
**Collection basis:** public government and allied-government records only. No classified access is claimed.

## Executive judgment

**Calibrated assessment: localized disruption risk.** The strongest current operational signal in this scan is Puerto Rico's continuing electricity-system emergency, documented in DOE orders issued 6 November and effective 8 November 2026. The orders cite unstable reserve capacity, forced outages, load-shedding events affecting at least 50,000 customers, widespread blackouts, and continuing transmission/distribution reliability problems. This is a real, geographically bounded civilian-service risk.

A separate joint cyber advisory published 8 October documents PRC government-linked cyber activity involving U.S. government, critical manufacturing, healthcare/public health, and IT victims. The advisory describes intrusion and sensitive-data theft; it does **not** report a coordinated nationwide destructive campaign or widespread outages. Executive Order 14421 is a formal national policy response to bulk-power equipment and supply-chain risk, not evidence that a grid attack is underway.

This scan found **no public evidence sufficient to assess an imminent nationwide disruption or a coordinated, active attack on multiple U.S. critical-infrastructure sectors**. That is a statement about the evidence located, not proof of safety.

## Material finding 1 — Puerto Rico electric-grid emergency renewed

- **What happened:** On 6 November 2026, DOE issued Order Nos. 202-25-1F and 202-25-2F to the Puerto Rico Electric Power Authority (PREPA). The generation order permits specified generation resources to operate for reliability; the transmission/distribution order continues vegetation and asset-management requirements. Both orders run from 8 November 2026 through 5 February 2027.
- **Who confirmed it:** U.S. Department of Energy; the orders are signed by Energy Secretary Chris Wright. DOE's 7 November newsroom release summarizes the renewal.
- **Event date vs publication date:** The underlying emergency is continuing from earlier orders dating to May 2025. The latest orders were issued 6 November and became effective 8 November; DOE's public summary was published 7 November.
- **Affected geography/sectors:** Puerto Rico's electric generation, transmission, and distribution system; the orders identify potential effects on residential customers, healthcare, industry, telecommunications, commerce, and other essential services.
- **Classification:** Policy action responding to an actual, ongoing grid-reliability emergency. This is not described as hostile activity or a cyberattack.
- **Evidence:** Order 1F states that the statutory emergency continues, cites unstable reserves, widespread blackouts, high forced-outage levels, and prior load-shedding events affecting at least 50,000 customers. Order 2F describes continuing transmission disruptions and distribution-system interruptions, while reporting that 2,186 miles of lines had been cleared by 22 October 2026.
- **Likely civilian implications:** Elevated localized risk of further interruptions to electricity and services dependent on power. The order does not establish that all Puerto Rico customers are currently without power, nor does it establish a new island-wide blackout on 6 November.
- **Confidence and unknowns:** High confidence that DOE made the statutory determination and that the orders document persistent system problems. Current reserve margins, the number of customers affected after 6 November, outage duration, and the precise distribution of risk across the island were not independently established in this scan.
- **Contradictory/mitigating evidence:** The orders are intended to preserve generation availability and improve line reliability. DOE reports progress on vegetation clearing. These measures mitigate risk; they do not remove the underlying reliability problem.
- **Primary sources:** [Generation Order 202-25-1F](https://www.energy.gov/documents/doe-order-no-202-25-1f); [Transmission and Distribution Order 202-25-2F](https://www.energy.gov/documents/doe-order-no-202-25-2f); [DOE summary, 7 November 2026](https://www.energy.gov/articles/energy-secretary-builds-progress-strengthen-puerto-ricos-energy-reliability).

## Material finding 2 — Joint advisory documents PRC-linked cyber intrusions and data theft

- **What happened:** Joint Cybersecurity Advisory AA26-281A describes PRC government-linked actors, enabled by Integrity Technology Group, combining automated scanning, botnet infrastructure, and hands-on exploitation to compromise networks and steal sensitive data. The FBI states that the advisory draws on technical evidence recovered from and observed during multiple FBI investigations.
- **Who confirmed it:** FBI, CISA, NSA, UK NCSC, Australian ASD ACSC, Canadian Centre for Cyber Security, Japan's National Police Agency and National Cybersecurity Office, New Zealand NCSC, and Spain's CNI.
- **Event date vs publication date:** The advisory was published 8 October 2026. It describes tool use dating back as early as 2017 and network access activity since at least mid-January 2021; individual victim incident dates are not publicly specified in the advisory.
- **Affected geography/sectors:** Victims in the United States and other countries. The U.S. sectors specifically named include government services and facilities, critical manufacturing, healthcare and public health, and information technology. The advisory also mentions law enforcement, education, and religious organizations.
- **Classification:** Confirmed hostile cyber activity, primarily network compromise, espionage, credential theft, and data exfiltration. It is not a report of destructive attacks or confirmed interruption of essential services.
- **Likely civilian implications:** Exposure of sensitive government, business, and healthcare data; possible downstream operational risk if compromised organizations rely on affected systems. The advisory does not identify a nationwide service outage or publicly quantify U.S. victims.
- **Confidence and unknowns:** High confidence that the multi-agency advisory and FBI investigative findings exist. The exact number and identities of affected U.S. organizations, the full geographic distribution, and any unreported operational consequences remain unknown.
- **Primary source:** [Joint Cybersecurity Advisory AA26-281A (FBI IC3 PDF)](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2026/261008.pdf).

## Material policy context — National emergency concerning bulk-power equipment

- **What happened:** Executive Order 14421, issued 26 August 2026, declared a national emergency concerning foreign-supplied bulk-power-system equipment and associated supply-chain risks. It authorizes DOE to restrict certain transactions when specified foreign-entity and national-security findings are made, and to consider mitigation or conditions for existing equipment.
- **Who confirmed it:** The White House; DOE's CESER office publishes an implementation page and public request-for-information materials.
- **Affected geography/sectors:** U.S. bulk-power generation and interconnected transmission equipment, including relevant control systems; the order excludes local electricity distribution facilities.
- **Classification:** National policy action and strategic threat determination. The order refers to potential backdoors, malicious remote access, cyber exploitation, and supply disruption, but it does not publicly identify a specific compromised U.S. utility, named equipment incident, or imminent attack.
- **Likely civilian implications:** Possible changes to procurement, vendor qualification, and equipment risk management over time. Implementation will need to balance security with replacement availability and continuity of service.
- **Confidence and unknowns:** High confidence in the order and its stated scope. DOE says implementing rules are due within 120 days of 26 August (approximately 24 December 2026); no final implementing rule was verified in this scan. Public evidence reviewed here does not establish how many installed systems, if any, have been identified for mitigation or replacement.
- **Primary sources:** [Executive Order 14421](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/08/declaring-a-national-emergency-to-secure-the-united-states-bulk-power-system/); [DOE implementation page](https://www.energy.gov/ceser/declaring-national-emergency-secure-united-states-bulk-power-system-executive-order).
