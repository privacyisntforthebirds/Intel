# Public threat watch — 2026-11-13

## Executive assessment

A material, non-hostile service-disruption event occurred on November 12 and continued into November 13: a broad Microsoft 365 / Exchange Online outage. Microsoft's incident updates describe service recovery work and state that the company has no evidence of malicious activity associated with the incident. The event affected widely used communications and collaboration services and some government and business operations, but this review did not establish a direct outage of U.S. electric, water, telecommunications-carrier, or other physical critical infrastructure.

No new public evidence of a coordinated nationwide destructive campaign or an imminent nationwide emergency was verified.

**Overall calibration:** localized / sector-specific disruption risk is supported; broader coordinated disruption remains a persistent strategic concern, not an established current campaign; evidence of imminent danger is not established.

## 1. Material development: Microsoft 365 / Exchange Online service disruption

- **Event start:** Microsoft's incident timeline records the Exchange Online incident as beginning at 2026-11-12 16:17 UTC (11:17 a.m. EST).
- **Publication / updates:** Microsoft began posting incident updates on November 12; updates continued through November 13.
- **Confirming source:** Microsoft 365 Status, through Exchange Online incident EX1550543 and broader Microsoft 365 advisory MO1550821. Associated Microsoft status updates are preserved in a public third-party status-history mirror; AP independently reported the outage and quoted Microsoft's public statements.
- **Initial geography:** Microsoft first described Exchange Online mailbox connectivity problems in the Americas, then said the issue could affect Microsoft 365 connectivity in any region.
- **Services / sectors:** Exchange Online and Outlook were directly affected; Microsoft updates also referenced impacts to additional Microsoft 365 services, including Teams, SharePoint, and Copilot. The outage disrupted communications, calendars, collaboration, file access, and dependent business and government workflows.
- **Operational effects:** Public reporting described impacts to businesses and some government operations worldwide. The public evidence reviewed here does not identify a confirmed U.S. federal agency or U.S. critical-infrastructure operator whose essential control operations were interrupted by this incident.
- **Classification:** Actual cloud-service / infrastructure outage. It is not a confirmed cyberattack, sabotage event, or hostile operation.
- **Cause and attribution:** Microsoft's updates described unexpectedly unavailable servers, elevated infrastructure load, server health-check and connection-processing problems, and traffic-management / routing mitigation. Microsoft stated in its November 13 updates that it had no evidence of malicious activity. A final root-cause determination was not yet available; Microsoft said it would publish a post-incident report after mitigation.
- **Recovery status at last detailed update reviewed:** A status-history mirror preserving Microsoft's updates showed a November 13 15:53 UTC (10:53 a.m. EST) update saying Microsoft was addressing residual impact and validating stable recovery across connection methods. It stated that Microsoft had no evidence of malicious activity and would publish a post-incident report after mitigation. This is a captured status update, not a claim that all tenants remained affected at the time this report was written.
- **Public status-page caveat:** Microsoft's public status landing page currently reports consumer products operational and directs business/enterprise users to the Microsoft 365 admin center for tenant-level health. The detailed business incident view is not fully available on the public landing page. Accordingly, complete tenant-by-tenant restoration cannot be independently confirmed from that page.
- **Civilian implications:** Temporary loss or delay of email, calendars, collaboration, file-sharing, and cloud-dependent workflows can impede government and business administration. Health, transport, and emergency effects should be attributed only where a specific organization or service confirms them; this review did not establish a U.S. frontline emergency-service failure.
- **Confidence:** High that a broad service disruption occurred and that Microsoft issued the cited incident updates; moderate on the exact aggregate number of affected users, complete service-by-service scope, and final root cause because Microsoft had not yet published its post-incident report.
- **Contradictory / mitigating evidence:** Microsoft reported improving service availability and no evidence of malicious activity. The public status landing page showed consumer products operational. These points indicate recovery and argue against treating the event as a continuing nationwide emergency, while not resolving the detailed business-tenant status question.
- **Primary / direct links:** [Microsoft service status](https://status.cloud.microsoft/); [Microsoft 365 Status account](https://x.com/MSFT365Status); [AP report on recovery progress, November 13](https://apnews.com/article/e2330e733db280805dbf7e3eb29ee0e1). A public mirror preserving Microsoft incident updates and timestamps is available at [StatusStack — Exchange Online incident EX1550543](https://www.statusstack.com/service/microsoftexchangeonline/incidents/01a1d0a2-9912-705b-8054-51d301f4d7e1).

## 2. Grid-reliability baseline correction: PJM / Wagner Unit 4 order

This is a previously issued order newly added to the current evidence ledger, not a new November 13 development.

- **Publication:** August 19, 2026.
- **Effective period:** August 20 through November 17, 2026.
- **Issuing authority:** U.S. Department of Energy under Section 202(c) of the Federal Power Act.
- **What the order does:** Permits PJM Interconnection, in coordination with Talen Energy, to continue operating Unit 4 at the Wagner Generating Station in Anne Arundel County, Maryland, beyond its existing operating limit.
- **DOE's stated basis:** PJM told DOE it expected to continue needing the unit during periods of high electricity demand to maintain reliable grid operations. DOE described the action as supporting reliability across the Mid-Atlantic PJM region.
- **Classification:** Grid-reliability policy action / temporary generation authorization. It is not a reported outage, hostile incident, or declaration that blackouts are imminent.
- **Civilian implications:** The order is intended to preserve generation availability and reduce outage risk during high-demand periods. Its approaching November 17 expiration is a monitoring point; no extension or replacement order was verified in this pass.
- **Confidence:** High for the order's existence, terms, dates, and DOE's stated rationale.
- **Source:** [DOE, Energy Secretary Acts to Protect Mid-Atlantic Grid, August 19, 2026](https://stage.energy.gov/articles/energy-secretary-acts-protect-mid-atlantic-grid).

## 5. Calibrated assessment

- **Localized disruption risk — supported.** The Microsoft outage demonstrates that failure of a widely used cloud provider can disrupt communications and workflows across many organizations and countries. The previously documented water-sector PLC incidents and Puerto Rico grid emergency remain part of the standing baseline.
- **Broader coordinated disruption risk — persistent strategic concern, not established as a current nationwide campaign.** Previously verified hostile cyber activity against U.S. infrastructure remains serious. The Microsoft outage itself is not evidence of hostile coordination; Microsoft stated it had no evidence of malicious activity.
- **Evidence of imminent danger — not established.** No verified public source reviewed in this pass identifies a specific imminent nationwide attack or emergency with a stated timeframe.

No extraordinary household action is indicated by this report. Proportionate readiness for ordinary service interruptions remains sensible.

## 6. Collection limitations

- CISA's main advisory index was not consistently accessible during this monitoring period; accessible official bulletins and agency pages were used where available.
- Microsoft's detailed Microsoft 365 business-tenant service-health information is primarily exposed through its admin center. The public landing page does not provide the full incident history.
- Some local Ohio records and allied-government source pages were not accessible or were not indexed in a way that allowed primary-document verification.
- Absence of a verified public report is not treated as proof that no incident occurred.

## Run-to-run conclusion

The new material event is the Microsoft 365 / Exchange Online outage beginning November 12 and continuing through a staged recovery on November 13. It is classified as a cloud-service outage, not a confirmed hostile cyber incident. The PJM/Wagner order is a baseline correction with a November 17 expiry to watch, not a new event. No new evidence of an imminent nationwide emergency was verified.
