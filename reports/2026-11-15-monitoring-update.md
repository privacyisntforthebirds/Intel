## 1. DOE Order No. 202-26-51 — J.H. Campbell Generating Plant, Michigan

- **Order issued:** November 13, 2026.
- **Public announcement:** November 14, 2026.
- **Effective period:** November 15, 2026 through February 12, 2027.
- **Confirming authority:** U.S. Department of Energy (DOE), under Section 202(c) of the Federal Power Act.
- **Affected entity / geography:** Midcontinent Independent System Operator (MISO); J.H. Campbell Generating Plant in West Olive, Michigan; Consumers Energy.
- **Classification:** Federal grid-reliability emergency policy action; not a hostile incident or a reported blackout.

The signed 14-page DOE order identifies Campbell as a 1,420 MW coal-fired generating facility. It directs MISO and Consumers Energy to take all measures necessary to keep the plant available to operate to meet the emergency. MISO is directed to use economic dispatch only during the hours necessary to meet the emergency and to minimize costs to ratepayers. MISO must provide daily notices to DOE concerning operation under the order and submit information on availability measures by November 28.

### DOE's stated risk basis

The order states that the Secretary has determined a statutory emergency exists in the MISO region due to increased electricity demand, shortage of electricity or generation/transmission facilities, and other causes. It describes tight reserve margins, accelerated demand growth (including large data-center loads), generator retirements, delays in new generation and transmission, and prior operating stress.

The order recounts that during Winter Storm Fern, MISO declared an Energy Emergency Alert 1 and an EEA 2 MaxGen event on January 24, 2026, due to generator outages, high demand, and transfer limits. It also cites a market-tool failure that reduced imports for approximately 12 hours. DOE states that the regional supply shortage is acute, that blackouts resulting from the shortfall are probable, and that such a loss of power could substantially harm public health and safety. These are DOE's formal findings supporting the order; they should be attributed to DOE rather than presented as an independently quantified forecast.

### Counterevidence and analytic qualification

The public record also contains important mitigating evidence:

- FERC, NERC, and Regional Entities reported on September 10 that the bulk-power system performed well during the 2026 Arctic cold period, with no significant disruptions to electric or natural-gas systems. Their report nevertheless said both systems were stretched over multiple days and that reliability challenges brought the system close to the edge.
- NERC's 2025 Long-Term Reliability Assessment classifies MISO as normal risk for 2026, elevated risk for 2027, and high risk beginning in 2028. DOE's order itself cites this assessment. These are planning risk categories, not a real-time outage forecast.
- On November 5, NERC reported that 99% of total net winter capacity in the continental United States had reported the ability to operate under extreme cold during winter 2025–2026. NERC also emphasized that winter reliability risks remain. This nationwide equipment-readiness metric does not by itself establish that MISO has sufficient energy to meet every future demand scenario.

These sources do not erase DOE's concern. They show that the order's severe legal finding should be read alongside broader reliability evidence and the distinction between equipment cold-weather capability, regional resource adequacy, and actual system outages.

### Civilian implications

The order is intended to preserve generation availability during periods of system stress and reduce the risk of supply shortfalls. It does not mean a blackout is occurring or scheduled. The plant's continued availability is a mitigation measure.

**Central Ohio relevance:** PJM states that its service territory includes Ohio. The new Campbell order is directed to MISO and a Michigan plant, not PJM or an Ohio utility. MISO and PJM are neighboring, interconnected regional operators, so regional conditions can interact through transfers, but the sources reviewed do not establish a direct local service impact in Grove City or Columbus.

**Confidence:** High that the order exists, its terms and effective dates, and DOE's stated rationale. Moderate regarding the probability, timing, and geographic extent of any future supply shortfall because the order does not provide a quantified real-time outage probability and independent planning assessments use different horizons and classifications.

## 2. FAA nationwide ground stop — November 13 technical explanation updated

- **Event date:** November 13, 2026.
- **New publication:** FAA Q&A published November 15, 2026.
- **Affected geography / sector:** Nationwide U.S. aviation; 17 of 22 Air Route Traffic Control Centers experienced instability, which spread to several Terminal Radar Approach Controls.
- **Classification:** Actual transportation-system service disruption; no hostile activity indicated by the FAA.

The FAA's November 15 Q&A provides new technical and operational detail. It says data flooded the FAA telecommunications network at a rate of approximately 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds. The initial investigation identifies two potential locations associated with the excessive data flooding: a radar site in Amarillo, Texas, and a radio communications site in El Dorado, Arkansas, where L3Harris had been deploying a new router.

The FAA says it blocked those locations, disabled the broadcasting function on new routers as a precaution, and stabilized the system. It says legacy TDM equipment became unstable; the root cause is still being investigated with L3Harris. The FAA says it has no indication of a cyberattack or outside malicious actor.

The FAA further states that all aircraft landed safely, all passengers arrived safely, and critical life-saving, law-enforcement, and military flights were not affected. Air traffic resumed at 8:15 p.m. Eastern on November 13, and operations are returning to normal.

**Assessment:** This is a material clarification of the November 14 baseline, not a new incident. The official FAA account strengthens the technical-failure explanation and narrows the candidate locations, while preserving uncertainty about the final root cause. The ground stop was a completed disruption; the FAA reports stable systems and continuing investigation.

## 5. Calibrated assessment

### Localized disruption risk — supported

DOE's repeated MISO emergency orders and current resource-adequacy findings support a continuing regional grid-reliability concern in the Midwest. The FAA event demonstrates a completed nationwide transportation-service disruption caused, on current evidence, by technical instability rather than hostile activity. Earlier verified water-sector incidents and Puerto Rico's grid emergency remain part of the baseline.

For central Ohio, the Campbell order is not a direct local warning because the facility is in MISO and central Ohio is in PJM. Existing PJM reliability orders remain relevant context, but no new central-Ohio outage or protective action was verified.

### Broader coordinated disruption risk — persistent strategic concern, not established as an active campaign

Previously documented state-linked cyber activity against U.S. infrastructure remains serious. The FAA event and the new DOE order do not indicate a coordinated hostile campaign. No evidence reviewed links recent outages or reliability actions to a common adversary.

### Evidence of imminent danger — not established

No verified public evidence reviewed in this pass establishes an imminent nationwide attack or emergency with a specific timeframe. DOE's severe regional reliability finding is important, but it is not a forecast of a scheduled blackout and should not be generalized to the entire country.

## 6. Collection limitations

- The FAA says its investigation with L3Harris is continuing; the November 15 Q&A is an updated preliminary account, not a final engineering report.
- DOE's order states the Secretary's legal determination and rationale; it does not provide a real-time probability or schedule for blackouts.
- NERC/FERC assessments address different periods and metrics. Equipment cold-weather readiness, seasonal resource adequacy, and immediate operating conditions are not interchangeable.
- Some CISA, Ohio state/local, and allied-government pages did not expose complete current records during this pass. No conclusion of safety is drawn from inaccessible sources.
- This assessment uses public information only and does not imply access to classified intelligence.
