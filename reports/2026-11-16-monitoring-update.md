## 1. United Kingdom: House of Lords national resilience report

- **Document:** *Are we ready? Building a culture of resilience*, HL Paper 79, National Resilience Committee.
- **Publication date:** 2026-11-16.
- **Event date:** The report assesses longstanding conditions and evidence gathered during the committee's 2026 inquiry; it does not describe a single new incident.
- **Issuer:** UK Parliament, House of Lords National Resilience Committee.
- **Classification:** Parliamentary risk assessment and policy recommendations; not an executive threat warning, exercise, or confirmed incident.
- **Geography/sectors:** United Kingdom; national and local emergency management, telecommunications, energy, water, food, medicines, transport, cyber, and supply chains.

### Principal findings

The committee judges that UK resilience arrangements are fragmented and insufficiently prepared for concurrent or cascading emergencies. It highlights:

- Inadequate integration of national, regional, local, private-sector, and voluntary-sector planning.
- Insufficiently tested backup communications for prolonged power outages and subsea-cable disruption.
- Dependence on international medicine and food supply chains, including committee-reported reliance on imported penicillin active ingredients.
- Concentration in supermarket food distribution.
- Gaps in cross-sector stress testing, cyber-resilience governance, and public communication.
- A need to publish performance against NATO's seven civil-preparedness baseline requirements.

The report also recognizes mitigating arrangements, including an eight-week buffer-stock requirement for secondary-care medicines and the establishment of a Supply Chain Centre. It recommends reviewing whether these arrangements are sufficient for prolonged disruption.

### Assessment

- **Civilian implications:** If the identified vulnerabilities are not addressed, a prolonged or compound disruption could place pressure on medicine availability, communications, food distribution, and local emergency response.
- **Confidence:** High that the committee made these findings and recommendations. Underlying statistical claims are attributed to the committee's report and have not all been independently re-measured in this monitoring pass.
- **Unknowns:** The UK Government's response, implementation timetable, funding, and whether recommendations will become binding requirements.
- **U.S. relevance:** Comparative warning indicators only. The report does not establish that the same vulnerabilities or supply-chain dependencies exist at the same scale in the United States.

## 2. United States and nine partners: San Francisco Declaration on AI supply-chain security

- **Signing date:** 2026-11-15.
- **Publication/announcement:** 2026-11-15; reported by Reuters on 2026-11-16.
- **Issuer/participants:** U.S. Department of State; governments of the United States, Australia, India, Israel, Japan, Republic of Korea, Qatar, Singapore, United Arab Emirates, and United Kingdom.
- **Classification:** International policy declaration and intended coordination framework; not an incident, threat warning, exercise, or binding joint export-control regime.
- **Scope:** Sensitive AI-related technologies and the physical/digital infrastructure supporting AI supply chains, including energy, ICT, communications, ports, transport, terminals, logistics, and digital platforms.

The declaration states that safeguarding sensitive technologies and supporting critical infrastructure is a shared endeavor. It proposes five working-group areas: pro-innovation policy frameworks, AI adoption in key sectors, supply-chain cooperation, safe harbors and supply-chain security hubs, and security/resilience.

### Assessment

- **Civilian implications:** Potential longer-term improvements in supply-chain security and resilience for AI-related infrastructure. No immediate change in civilian service availability is established.
- **Confidence:** High that the declaration was signed and that the State Department announced its contents; Reuters independently reported the signatories and framework.
- **Important limitation:** The declaration does not itself publish a binding protected-technology list, create new joint export controls, identify compromised infrastructure, or name a specific hostile actor. The proposed working groups' implementation details, budgets, and timelines remain to be established.
- **Context:** Reuters situates the initiative within strategic technology competition with China. That context should not be confused with a specific finding of hostile activity in the declaration itself.
